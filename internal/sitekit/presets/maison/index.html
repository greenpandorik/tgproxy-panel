A ground-floor room in Ghent that took eleven years to finish

Words by Elin Vandeputte. Photographed in March.

Marthe Dekeyser bought the house in 2014 for the plaster. Everything else was wrong — a partition wall through the middle of the front room, a boiler where the fireplace had been, and a floor laid over a floor laid over brick. But two metres of original cornice had survived above the window, and she decided that was enough to start from.

She worked backwards. The partition came out in the first month; the second floor covering took four years, because she lifted it a board at a time on weekends and kept the ones that were sound. What is down there now is roughly sixty per cent of the original pine, laid loose in the same direction, unsealed. It marks. She says that is the point of it.

"I stopped buying things for the room in the third year. After that it was mostly moving what was already here until it stopped bothering me."

The furniture is thin on the ground. A long oak table by a maker in Kortrijk, bought unfinished and oiled at home. Two chairs found in a barn near Ronse, reglued by a restorer who charged eighty euros and told her not to strip them. A wall lamp from a hospital in Bruges, rewired.

What holds the room together is not the objects but the amount of nothing between them. The far corner is empty. There is a stretch of wall three metres wide with a single nail in it, and she has hung nothing there since 2019. When we asked why, she said the light lands on it at four o'clock and she would rather look at that.
